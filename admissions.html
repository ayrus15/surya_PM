Third case study

Auth — Admissions Workflow Management Platform

QR-enabled candidate verification, checkpoint routing, and real-time admissions visibility for Christ University's interview-day operations.

Status: deployed & functional — not adopted for live rollout that academic year Stack: TypeScript · Node.js · Supabase · QR + messaging integrations

Read this first

Auth was built, deployed, and tested end-to-end using sample records and real academic-year applicant data, and demonstrated to the Admissions Cell. The university did not proceed with the intended live rollout that year because the dedicated QR-scanning devices and display infrastructure needed for a well-organized, high-throughput deployment couldn't be arranged in time. This was an operational-readiness constraint, not a claim that the application was nonfunctional.

The problem

A manual candidate journey, prone to risk

Interview days involve many candidates moving through a sequence of locations and activities within assigned time slots. The existing process depended on volunteers manually checking application cards, verifying candidate details, directing people to the correct block or floor, and recording attendance on paper. That created several operational risks:

  • Repeated manual verification at different points in the candidate journey.
  • Candidates needing to ask volunteers where to assemble, which floor to visit, or what came next.
  • Limited real-time visibility into how many candidates had reached each checkpoint.
  • Paper attendance sheets that could be incomplete when signatures were missed.
  • Manual coordination between admissions staff, volunteers, and interview panels.
  • Difficulty assigning a second faculty member as a co-panelist for interviews.
Product goal

A controlled candidate flow, not just faster scanning

Create a QR-driven workflow that verifies each candidate, directs them to the next required location, records progression through the admissions journey, and gives the Admissions Cell a consolidated operational view. The intended outcome was a more controlled candidate flow with fewer manual handoffs and more reliable attendance records — not merely faster scanning.

Candidate journey

Five checkpoints, one consolidated view

1

Application & QR identity

Candidate application information loaded in bulk from existing records (CSV/Excel). A QR code on the application card is the scan point for lookup and verification.

2

Campus entry

The QR scan verifies the candidate against the record and can trigger a welcome message with assembly instructions.

3

Block / floor routing

A scan shows course, floor, and room details on a connected display; SMS/WhatsApp can also direct the candidate to the next location.

4

Written assessment entry

A further scan records entry into the assessment stage, giving visibility into how many candidates from a batch have reached that point.

5

Marks verification

A checkpoint in the candidate's progression; marks verification itself is carried out by the Admissions Cell, not by the application — the scan only records that the candidate has reached this stage.

6

Interview panel

Records candidate attendance/completion, reducing dependence on a paper signature sheet that could be missed.

Additional workflow: faculty co-panel assignment

Interview panels consisted of two faculty members. Assigning a second faculty member as co-panelist was a separate coordination problem in the existing process — the application included a selection and assignment workflow to make that step easier to manage.

My contribution

Discovery, workflow definition, and selected components

I drew on my own experience with admissions operations and volunteer-led candidate handling, then spoke with the Admissions Cell to understand the process and its pain points.

  • Mapped the end-to-end candidate journey and identified where QR checkpoints could improve verification and routing.
  • Translated admissions-team requirements into a multi-stage workflow.
  • Contributed to SMS and WhatsApp communication integration for next-step instructions.
  • Designed the faculty co-panel selection and assignment logic.
  • Helped structure bulk-imported applicant data; incomplete records required cleaning before use.
  • Participated in end-to-end testing and demonstrations with the Admissions Cell.

I built Auth together with a collaborator, and this case study focuses on the parts I worked on directly.

Validation & readiness

Tested end-to-end, demonstrated, then shelved for hardware — not product — reasons

  • Verified application behavior with sample records and the academic-year applicant data provided.
  • Ran end-to-end test scenarios across the intended workflow.
  • Demonstrated the application to the Admissions Cell.
  • Kept the application deployed and accessible after the intended rollout window; it currently holds the previous academic year's applicant data and would need refreshed data for a new cycle.

The application was developed late in the planning timeline, leaving insufficient time to arrange the dedicated QR scanners and multiple display screens the event needed. The university preferred a highly organized, reliable setup over a partial rollout on ordinary phones or laptops at every checkpoint — which could technically work, but risked recreating the exact queues and congestion the system was built to solve.

The system was designed to support admissions at institutional scale — the university had more than 16,000 applicants in its broader admissions operation that year, though Auth itself was used for a smaller pilot slice of that process.

Limitations & future improvements

What's next, if revived

  • Explicit handling for slot changes, multiple applications by the same candidate, and candidates arriving outside their assigned time (identified as future-development areas, not implemented features).
  • Validate mandatory fields and applicant records before import, to reduce manual data cleaning.
  • Design a staged hardware plan early — device procurement, display placement, connectivity, event-day support.
  • A controlled fallback mode for connectivity or scanner failure, with reconciliation once service returns.
  • Measure checkpoint wait times and batch throughput so operational teams can adjust staffing and release timing.
  • Refresh applicant data and revalidate course/slot/routing rules before any future admissions cycle.
Key learning

A functional application is only one part of an operational product

For a high-volume, time-sensitive university process, rollout readiness depends as much on hardware availability, stakeholder alignment, data quality, exception handling, and a realistic implementation timeline as on the software itself. The project reinforced planning the technical deployment and the physical operating environment as one integrated system — not sequentially.
